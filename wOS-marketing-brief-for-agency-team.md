## The positioning (three levels)

### Level 1: Soft (awareness)
"We wrote the open behavioral standard for AI agents."
Audience: LinkedIn, conferences, press. Goal: establish authority.

### Level 2: Marketing (interest)
"Our agents are built on wOS — that's why they don't hallucinate, don't ghost-complete, and don't apologize instead of fixing."
Audience: prospects evaluating wgnr.ai. Goal: differentiate from agencies that bolt on AI without discipline.

### Level 3: Business (decision)
"We'll audit your current agent deployments against wOS conformance and show you exactly where they're failing."
Audience: qualified leads. Goal: convert interest into engagement.

## Customer-fit profile

The prospect who buys wgnr.ai because of wOS:

- **Running AI agents in production** — not just experimenting with ChatGPT
- **Experiencing trust issues** — agents producing unreliable work, stakeholders losing confidence in AI outputs, retry loops burning budget
- **Already spending on AI infrastructure** — models, APIs, orchestration tools — but not getting consistent results
- **Decision-maker:** CTO, VP Engineering, Head of AI, Director of Operations, or founder at a tech-forward company
- **Company size:** mid-market to enterprise (100–5,000 employees)
- **Industry:** universal — the behavioral problem is model-agnostic and industry-agnostic
- **Trigger event:** a high-visibility agent failure (customer-facing error, data loss, compliance issue, budget overrun from retry loops)

## What NOT to do

- Don't sell wOS itself. The spec is free. The expertise is the product.
- Don't position wOS as competing with ACS, AGENTS.md, or Agent OS. Complementary only.
- Don't use "AI governance" language — that's the enterprise compliance framing (SOC 2, ISO 27001). wOS is "behavioral design" — different category, different buyer.
- Don't target developers as the primary buyer for services. Developers adopt the spec. CTOs and VPs buy the audit.
- Don't promise specific outcomes from the audit until the assessment framework is built. The audit identifies gaps; it doesn't guarantee fixes.
